FAQ

Common questions

What do "15-minute RPO" and "15-minute RTO" mean?

RPO (Recovery Point Objective) is the most data you could lose in a disaster — here, at most about 15 minutes of changes. RTO (Recovery Time Objective) is how long it takes to be back up and running — here, a target of about 15 minutes. Both are validated with scheduled failover drills.

Do we really not need Oracle Enterprise Edition?

For disaster recovery and reporting replicas, you don't need Enterprise Edition. Oracle's built-in route for these features is Data Guard / Active Data Guard, which require Enterprise Edition licensing. CloudZ13 engineers a custom solution that delivers these outcomes on Oracle Standard Edition 2 running on Linux.

How much will we actually save?

It depends on your server sizing, region and any negotiated Oracle discounts. As an illustration, list prices for a 16-core server are about $1,128,000 for Enterprise Edition plus Active Data Guard, versus about $35,000 for Standard Edition 2 — before support fees. We'll prepare a free comparison for your actual environment.

How do you access our systems?

All services are delivered remotely over secure VPN connections. You provide and control secure access to your database environments.

Which platforms and clouds do you support?

We are Linux-first and work across on-premise environments as well as AWS, Azure and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI).

What happens if there's a critical outage at 3 a.m.?

Retainer clients get 24×7 Sev-1 coverage with guaranteed response times and a dedicated senior DBA point of contact. We fix the immediate problem, then follow up with root-cause analysis and a permanent fix.
